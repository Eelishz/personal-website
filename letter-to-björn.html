Letter to Björn

Preamble

I'm currently sitting in our very nice AirBnB room in Barcelona typing this up on the couch. I don't know how long this will be, but I know it will be long. Maybe you will take the time to read this and even listen to some the albums at some point.

This list is more on less a brain-dump. Records on here are either things which were top-of-mind in Vic or albums which have somehow been formative or important to me during my life.

A good majority of it is elitist and pretentious shit--I hope you will forgive me.

One album per artist/group!

The list is in no particular order, except the order in which things came to mind.

Album: Наркологический университет миллионов

Artist: Sektor Gaza

Favourite track: Pora Domoy

Starting off with a bang with some Russian punk rock. Based on the lyrics which I've either translated or which Russian speaking friends have explained to me, this album runs the gamut between complete joke songs and serious political commentary.

Yehor is also a fan!

Album: Techno Pop

Artist: Kraftwerk

Favourite track: Telephone Call

Kraftwerk is such an interesting group. One of these bands where it really could have been any album, but I picked this one. We spoke briefly about this record, but I'm not sure how much you remember.

The long and the short of it is that Kraftwerk have always been (and still are!) on the cutting fucking edge of music technology and this album is the one where they got their hands on a sampler. Crazy shit on this record!

Kraftwerk also understands the art of short albums, which I really appreciate!

This record is rewarding of a good sound system, especially in the bass and imaging departments, but unlike some of their other work, doesn't outright require it.

For what it's worth, here's my current Kraftwerk top 3:

  1. Techno Pop
  2. Computer World
  3. Autobahn (an older and very different sound, but also very cool! The title track is worth a listen.)

Album: Olisimpa täällä (translation: "I Wish I Was Here")

Artist: Saimaa

Favourite track: Ylös Alas Outo Lumo

Standout lyric:

Olen odottanut kärsivällisesti

Niin kärsivällisesti

Sitä että elämä alkaa

Et se alkais jo oikeesti

Rough translation:

I've waited patiently

So patiently

For life to start

For it to start for real

Saimaa is a Finnish prog rock (I guess?) group which is made up of, most notably, the lead guitarist of Tehosekoitin (translation: "Blender"), a Finnish rock group from the 90s, as well as other hella talented musicians.

Side note: If you're interested in Tehosekoitin, "Kaikki nuoret tyypit" (translation: "All the Young Dudes". Yes, I know this is an English language track as well. Yes, I do like it.) and "Ja maailma on sun" (translation: "And the World is Yours") are worth checking out!

This particular album features Pyhimys (translation: "Saint") a Finnish rapper. It is literally rapping over prog beats--crazy shit. For whatever reason this album is super emotional for me. Maybe it's the subject matter or maybe it's the language, but by the time "Styroxista rakennettu talo" (translation: "House Made of Styrofoam") starts playing, I'm usually on the verge of tears.

Side note: "Styroxista rakennettu talo" is a clear homage to one of the most important Finnish 90s groups of all time: Ultra Bra. This band holds a sort of very important place in the hearts of a certain generation of Finns. Not entirely my jam, but "Sinä lähdit pois" (translation: "You Went Away") and "Jäätelöauto" (translation: "Ice Cream Truck") are worth checking out. That last one is a certified banger!

Honorable mention: if you want something less rap and more prog/jazz/fusion craziness, "Matka mielen ytimeen" (translation: "Journey to the Center of the Mind") is worth checking out. The album is a collection of cover versions of Finnish classics done in an over the top jazz fusion style. "Levoton tuhkimo" (translation: "Restless Cinderella") is worth checking out at the very least. Both the original and the Saimaa versions are fantastic.

Obligatory "I've seen them live" here as well.

Album: Journey to the Centre of the Earth

Artist: Rick Wakeman

Favourite track: The Storm

Standout lyric:

Crystals of opaque quartz, studded limpid tears,

Forming magic chandeliers, lighting blistered galleries.

This is a late addition to the list, but "Journey to the Center of the Mind" got me thinking about this. This was actually originally introduced to me by my dad.

What can I say about this record? First of all, Rick Wakeman is such a fucking character. As far as I can tell, he invented the whole "keyboard wizzard" gimmick. He is so bombastic and so over the top, but it never seems disingenuous. The album itself is an adaptation of the book with the same name. Yes, it's an album based on a book! It's got several singers, a rock band, a full fucking symphony orchestra, and of course gratuitous keyboard parts.

I'm not saying this is good, per se, but it's not bad either.

Album: V

Artist: Spock's Beard

Favourite track: At the End of the Day

Standout lyric:

At the end of the day

It's what you do not what you say

At the end of the day

You'll be fine

This band is a bit weird about licensing, so no Spotify link is available :\

Spock's Beard is one of these bands which is objectively a bit cringe, but I still like them. They are part of this whole neo-prog revival thing that was going on in the 90s. The long and the short of it is that Neal Morse (guitarist, lead singer) is a fucking genius singer songwriter, even if he is making evangelical rock these days... Also an insanely prolific musician who seems to always be involved in no less than 5 groups or projects at the same time. If nothing else, go listen to "At the End of the Day". I won't spoil the lyrical content.

My mom says it sounds like music from the future, if that's any indication.

Album: Bodies and Souls

Artist: Manhattan Transfer

Favourite track: American Pop feat. Frankie Valli

This was a recommendation from a friend and a good one at that! Thank you Juha! This album toes the line between complex jazz chord changes and the cheesiest 80s pop you've ever heard.

Give it a spin!

Album: Stranger Head Prevail

Artist: Thank You Scientist

Favourite track: Mr. Invisible

Standout lyric:

What are you waiting for?

Another friend recommendation, although I was aware of this band before my friend Vili finally forced me to actually listen to this record.

I think the best argument I can present for this band is their current instrument lineup:

  • Electric guitar
  • Violin
  • Trumpet
  • Saxopohone
  • Drums
  • Vocals
  • Bass

And they play what is essentially a mixture of jazz and metal...

Crazy solos as well.

Album: Phoenix

Artist: Dirty Loops

Favourite track: Work Shit Out

This, and I guess a lot of other groups on this list, are what you might call "musician's music". Shit that appeals to nerds, basically. With that being said, go listen to this album. It's like pop, but also overplayed like hell. In a good way. Excellent music videos available for the whole album!

Album: We Like it Here

Artist: Snarky Puppy

Favourite track: Lingus

More of the same as above, I guess. Yet more white people jazz, I know, but genuinely worth a spin. The normies also seem to appreciate this one.

Also, I've seen them live and they're excellent!

Album: Toilet

Artist: Clown Core

Favourite track: Toilet

Standout lyric:

Don't you ever turn my pizza inside out

*Clown horn*

Calzone will not be tolerated

*Clown horn*

You have no idea what I'm capable of

Clown Core is an anonymous novelty band who have put out two very short EPs. Crazy insane technical ability disguised behind clown masks and funny music videos. Both of their records are visual albums meaning that every track has a corresponding music video which you can watch on YouTube.

The sax solos on this one are crazy, and the fact the that drummer (who definitely isn't Lewis Cole of Knower fame) can play keyboards and drums at the same time is bonkers.

Album: Deja Vu

Artist: Crosby, Stills, Nash & Young

Favourite track: Carry On

Standout lyric:

One morning I woke up and I knew

You were really gone

A new day, a new way

And new eyes to see the dawn

Go your way, I'll go mine

And carry on

Immaculate vibes with this album and interesting history with the group as a whole. I'm a real sucker for the whole singing in harmony thing.

Album: The Sunset Tree

Artist: The Mountain Goats

Favourite track: This Year

Standout lyric:

I am gonna make it through this year if it kills me

I am gonna make it through this year if it kills me

I don't know how to describe The Mountain Goats, but when you listen to their music it's impossible not to have some kind of emotional reaction.

The song No Children is also worth checking out from them.

Album: Blackwater Park

Artist: Opeth

Favourite track: Bleak

So I guess this list had to have at least one serious metal album on it. Don't ask me what a "serious metal album" is, but I'm pretty sure this would qualify.

Despite the whole grunge-y metal aesthetic, this is one the most beautiful sounding albums out there in my opinion. Insane amounts of clarity sparkle in the recording. Incidentally, this album was produced by Steven Wilson, who seems to be a sort of recurring character in my musical life.

Album: In Absentia

Artist: Porcupine Tree

Favourite track: Trains

Standout lyric:

A walk in the woods and I will try

Something under the trees that made you cry

It's so erotic when your make up runs

I had trouble picking which Porcupine Tree album, but landed on In Absentia.

This is another one of these bands which I probably only like because they're what I was listening to when I was 16, 17, and 18.

The songs are absolutely beautiful though and, at the very least, the one linked here is worth a listen.

While I have never personally seen Porcupine Tree live, an old teacher of mine had, and reported it to be "the most boring gig I've ever been to". So, there's that!

Album: Bridge Over Troubled Water

Artist: Simon & Garfunkel

Favourite track: Baby Driver

Paul Simon and Art Garfunkel are fucking musical geniuses. I mean, talk about nominative determinism!

Album: Desire

Artist: Bob Dylan

Favourite track: Hurricane

Standout lyric:

Your sister sees the future like your mama and yourself

You've never learned to read or write, there's no books upon your shelf

And your pleasure knows no limits, your voice is like a meadow lark

But your heart is like an ocean, mysterious and dark

Dylan is one of these people who I did not really get for a long time.

However, it seems that I've finally become enough of an old man to understand his music. Incredible shit.

Album: Can't Buy a Thrill

Artist: Steely Dan

Favourite track: Brooklyn (Owes The Charmer Under Me)

Standout lyric:

You been tellin' me you're a genius since you were seventeen

In all the time I've known you, I still don't know what you mean

The weekend at the college didn't turn out like you planned

The things that pass for knowledge, I can't understand

It could have been any Dan album, but it ended up being this one.

This record has in spades that shit that I'm really into. Happy music with depressing lyrics. All of Steely Dan's albums actually have this immaculate mid-life-crisis energy to them that's just so good if you yourself are going through something as well.

The later stuff and even the Donald Fagen solo works are cool in their own way, but I really like the 70s rock incarnation of Steely Dan.

Album: Vinne Colaiuta

Artist: Vinne Colaiuta

Favourite track: I'm Tweeked / Attack Of The 20lb Pizza

It's like jazz, but crazy.

Album: The Yes Album

Artist: Yes

Favourite track: Starship Trooper

Standout lyric:

Loneliness is a power that we possess

To give or take away forever

All I know can be shown by your acceptance

Of the facts there shown before you

Take what I say in a different way

And it's easy to say that this is all confusion

As I see a new day in me

I can also show it you, and you may (follow)

It could have been any 70s Yes album, but it ended up being this one.

I'm so in love with Yes. They somehow manage to do the whole over the top prog thing without it ever coming of as pretentious or like they're trying to make high art. Jon Anderson's lyrics are also fantastic!

Album: The Money Store

Artist: Death Grips

Favourite track: I've seen footage

My journey with Death Grips starts with this weird Soundcloud music scene that was happening around 2016.

I don't know exactly how to describe it, but the songs were like ironic mashups often featuring a specific set of recurring songs. Death Grips was one of these artists that the community loved to meme on, so I got exposed.

Slowly, but surly, "wtf is this?" turned into ironic enjoyment, which turned into genuine enjoyment.

Album: Flood

Artist: They Might Be Giants

Favourite track: Birdhouse in Your Soul

Standout lyric:

A woman came up to me and said "I'd like to poison your mind

With wrong ideas that appeal to you, though I am not unkind"

She looked at me, I looked at something written across her scalp

And these are the words that it faintly said as I tried to call for help:

This album was so fucking ahead of its time--it's not even funny.

All the songs on here sound like they were made for the internet, if that makes any sense. Every now and then some 90s sounding pop-rock track will sneak in and you'll remember what you're actually listening to, but the rest of the time it's all these short little songs with absurdist lyrics. The song Particle Man sounds like it could have been an actual TikTok trend from circa 2022.

My dad said it sounds like Zappa, which is an interesting and probably not entirely incorrect read.

Album: Abbey Road

Artist: The Beatles

Favourite track: Octopus's Garden

Standout lyric:

And in the end, the love you take

Is equal to the love you make

The story with this album is that a friend of mine in my last year of high school came up to me and told me in no uncertain terms that The Beatles' Abbey Road is a work of musical genius and I should listen to it immediately.

I was 18 years old then and only actually listened to the record when I was 24, so you do the math on that. I think needless to say my friend was right and Abbey Road is a work of musical genius, although I'm not sure I would have been ready for it in high school, if you know what I mean.

Album: To Pimp A Butterfly

Artist: Kendrick Lamar

Favourite track: For Free?

Standout lyric:

I remember you was conflicted

Misusing your influence

I will admit that I am 10 years late to this album. It got heavily recommended to me back when it originally came out, but I never really gave it a fair chance.

TPAB is something like 90 minutes long, which as far as I'm concerned, is too long. One night though I did finally manage to make my way through the entire album and I can say with certainty that it is that good.

This album does the Abbey Road trick. It starts with all these light pop numbers and then hits you in the second half. If Abbey Road makes you understand The Beatles, TPAB makes you understand Kendrick.

Album: Images and Words

Artist: Dream Theater

Favourite track: Learning to Live

Standout lyric:

Before the leaves have fallen

Before we lock the doors

There must be the third and last dance, this one will last forever

Metropolis watches and thoughtfully smiles, she's taken you to your home

It can only take place, when the struggle between our children has ended

Now the Miracle and the Sleeper know

That the third is love, love is the dance of eternity!

This album comes with a story, which is great because it means I actually have something substantive to say!

I was probably 15 or 16 years old taking the long way home walking along Helsinki's coast line when I got a text from a friend. I have since lost those messages, but the only content that I'm sure was included was a link to a song called Metropolis - Part I.

Those next nine and a half minutes would go on to define my musical identity for the rest of my teenage years. Needless to say, it was some of the most mind blowing shit I'd ever heard.

I have seen them live on multiple occasions and, in a sense, they are very good, but the second show was one of the stinkiest gigs I've ever been to!

Album: Moving Pictures

Artist: Rush

Favourite track: YYZ

Standout lyric:

All the world's indeed a stage

We are merely players

Performers and portrayers

Each another's audience outside the gilded cage

Another case of "well if I had to pick one". Rush is one of these bands which has slowly kinda grown on me. I've started to appreciate them more as I've gotten older.

I happen to think that Moving Pictures is their tightest package, but some of the proggier stuff is neat as well!

Album: Passion and Warfare

Artist: Steve Vai

Favourite track: The Riddle

Standout lyric:

We may be human,

But we're still animals

What you have to understand about me and this album is that I was a teenager and I wanted to get good at guitar. There are a few usual suspects people gravitate towards: Joe Satriani, Nuno Bettencourt, Eddie Van Halen, etc. For me, it was John Petrucci and Steve Vai.

I don't like this album because of its musical content, although it is at times very good. I like this album because of who is playing on it and what it represents.

I should also mention here that I have seen Mr. Vai live and not only is he an excellent showman (there were costume changes!), but his music is somehow far better live than it is on the recording. If I were to try to reverse engineer what's going on, I would probably say that for most of the time his rhythm section was holding down a solid groove and Vai was just improvising. They play the main melody, what jazz musicians would call the "head", every now and then, and it all sorta works as a cohesive whole. It's like jazz.

does anybody really know what time it is?

One of the funkiest albums of all time! Really good recording as well considering that this is from 1969!!!

Chicago as a band is super interesting and their whole discography is worth checking out. Also one of these bands which managed to completely reinvent their sound in the 80s.

Album: Random Access Memories

Favourite track: Giorgio by Moroder

This album is almost too good. It's like these two French fuckers got together in the studio and said "hey, for our last project let's just make something totally irresistible to music nerds." and they totally pulled it off.

It's perfect, all of it.

Album: Young Americans

You know the drill. It could have been pretty much any Bowie record, but it ended up being this one.

For what it's worth, this one gets points for (as far as I'm aware) also being my dad's favorite.

Album: OK Computer

Artist: Radiohead

Favourite track: Paranoid Android

Standout lyric:

In a fast German car

I'm amazed that I survived

An airbag saved my life

My friend from high school, the one telling me to listen to Abbey Road, was also telling me to listen to OK Computer.

If I sorta kinda probably wasn't ready for Abbey Road in high school, I definitely would not have been ready for OK Computer. This record has a sort of emotional rawness which has really taken a while for me to appreciate. The recordings themselves are also absolutely beautiful.

Man, I should really take people recommendations when they use phrases like "best album ever"...

Album: Mezzanine

I'm not really up on any of the Massive Attack lore. I don't know the history of the group (?), but what I do know is that this album sounds goddamn beautiful.

Album: Brothers in Arms

Favourite track: Why Worry

Standout lyric:

Was this album a glorified tech demo for Compact Disk? Maybe? Does it sound incredibly fucking good? Yes.

I can add yet another record to the pile of shit that really didn't make any sense to me before I got a good sound system. This is the type of thing you really have to turn up loud.

Album: Breakfast in America

Artist: Supertramp

Favourite track: Take The Long Way Home

Standout lyric:

So, you think you're a Romeo

Playing a part in a picture show

Well, take the long way home

Take the long way home

Supertramp is such an underrated band. All of it is good, their entire discography.

They were doing the whole English prog rock thing, but with their own sort of twist to it. Needless to say, this particular album also sounds fucking amazing and is a real testament to the recording technology of the time.
